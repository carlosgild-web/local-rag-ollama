Urban gardening makes use of small spaces such as balconies, windowsills,
and rooftops to grow vegetables, herbs, and flowers where a traditional
backyard garden is not available.

Container choice matters a lot in small spaces. Pots need drainage holes,
and deeper containers work better for root vegetables like carrots, while
shallow trays are fine for lettuce and herbs. Lightweight potting mix
drains better than garden soil and is usually recommended for containers.

Sunlight is often the biggest limiting factor in cities, since tall
buildings can shade a balcony for much of the day. Herbs like mint and
parsley tolerate partial shade reasonably well, while tomatoes and
peppers need several hours of direct sun to produce a good harvest.

Watering container plants more often than ground plants is normal
because pots dry out faster. Checking the soil with a finger before
watering helps avoid both overwatering and letting plants wilt.
