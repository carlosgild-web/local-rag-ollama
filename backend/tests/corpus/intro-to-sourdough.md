# Introduction to Sourdough Bread

Sourdough bread relies on a wild culture of yeast and lactic acid bacteria
instead of commercial yeast. The culture, called a starter, is a mix of
flour and water that is fed regularly until it becomes active and bubbly.

## Building a Starter

To build a starter from scratch, combine equal parts flour and water in a
jar and leave it at room temperature. Discard half and feed it fresh flour
and water every 24 hours. After five to seven days it should double in
size within a few hours of feeding, which means it is ready to bake with.

## Mixing and Fermentation

Sourdough dough is mixed with less force than commercial yeast dough and
then left to ferment slowly, often for many hours. This slow fermentation
develops flavor and makes the bread easier to digest for some people.

## Shaping and Baking

Once the dough has risen, it is shaped into a tight ball or oval, left to
rest a final time, and then baked at high heat, usually inside a covered
pot that traps steam and helps the crust turn crisp and golden.
