There are many ways to brew coffee, and each method changes the taste
of the final cup even when starting from the same beans.

Pour-over brewing uses a cone-shaped filter and a slow, controlled
stream of hot water poured in stages. It tends to produce a clean,
bright cup that highlights a coffee's acidity and aromatic qualities.

French press brewing steeps coarse coffee grounds directly in hot water
for several minutes before pressing a metal mesh filter down to separate
the grounds. Because no paper filter is used, more oils stay in the cup,
giving a heavier, fuller body.

Espresso forces hot water through finely ground, tightly packed coffee
at high pressure in a short amount of time. The result is a small,
concentrated shot with a thick layer of foam on top called crema.

Cold brew steeps coarse grounds in room-temperature or cold water for
many hours, producing a smooth, low-acid concentrate that is usually
diluted before drinking.
