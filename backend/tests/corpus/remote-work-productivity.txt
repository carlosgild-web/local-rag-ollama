Working from home removes a commute but introduces its own challenges
for staying focused and separating work from personal life.

A dedicated workspace, even a small desk in a corner, helps signal to
your brain that it is time to work. Closing a laptop or leaving the room
at the end of the day can serve as a similar signal that work has
ended.

Scheduling focused blocks of time for demanding tasks, and grouping
smaller tasks like email into set windows rather than checking
constantly, tends to reduce the feeling of being busy all day without
making real progress.

Regular breaks away from the screen, including a short walk if
possible, help maintain concentration over a full day better than
working straight through without pauses.

Clear communication with teammates about availability and response
times matters more remotely than in an office, where presence alone
used to signal availability.
